Zap of Nakamoto · Round 2 · Direction “The Zap”

One gesture. Tap, zap, done.

The brand is built around a single moment: money leaving one phone and arriving on another in a blink. Everything else stays out of the way. The mark, the colour and the motion all tell that one story.

1 · The mark

Three ways to draw a zap

Each one leaves the coin-and-bolt behind. Almost every Lightning wallet uses that, so it says “crypto app” and not “Zap of Nakamoto”.

2 · Colour

Leave Bitcoin orange to everyone else

Nearly every bitcoin app is orange. Since the name is fixed and the colour isn't, colour is the cheapest way to be recognised in a store full of orange icons.

3 · Wordmark

Say “Zap”, keep “of Nakamoto” small

People will call the app “Zap”. The lockup leads with that word and lets the rest of the name sit quietly underneath.

One moment of motion, nothing else

The app stays still and quiet. Only a payment moves: the mark flashes over the balance, the number ticks up, and a short line says who it came from. That moment is the brand. (Example data.)

Zap is the verb

“Zap” replaces send and pay wherever a sentence allows it. Buttons can stay “Send” and “Receive” so first-timers aren't puzzled.

5 · Marketing read

Why this direction works for first-timers

Small amounts are a feature

Beginners worry about losing money. A playful brand says “this is for pocket change” without a warning label. ₿1,000 feels like a lot of coins, not a fraction of something scary.

Look different on the shelf

Zapstore and phone home screens are full of orange coins. A yellow, violet or cobalt square with a Z is recognisable at a glance.

The verb does the marketing

“Zap me” is easy to say out loud at a bar or a meetup. Every payment repeats the brand name. That's free word of mouth.

The risk: too toy-like

A playful look can make money feel unsafe. We balance it with calm screens, plain words and one strong motion. The fun stays in the zap and isn't everywhere.
